Tire sur la neige is hot maple syrup poured onto fresh snow, then rolled onto a stick as it turns into chewy taffy. It is the most Québécois thing you can eat in a Montréal winter.

Manger à McGill brings the whole campus together for one big moment: 600 students, one lawn, one bite. Bring a friend, bring a mitten, bring a sweet tooth.

Make your own maple taffy

Three ingredients' worth of effort, one big payoff. Check things off as you go.

You'll need

Makes about 3 to 4 strips of taffy.

How to do it

  1. Pack the snow or ice tightly into a tray and keep it outside or in the freezer until the very last second.
  2. Pour the syrup into the saucepan. Use a deep one, because it foams up as it boils.
  3. Heat over medium heat without stirring until the thermometer reads 112 to 115 °C (234 to 240 °F), the soft ball stage.
  4. Take it off the heat right away and let the bubbles settle for a few seconds.
  5. Drizzle the syrup in thin lines across the snow. It will set in seconds.
  6. Press a stick at the end of a line and roll it up like a tiny scroll.
  7. Eat it immediately, while it is cold and chewy.
Hot syrup burns. Keep the pot away from the crowd and let one person do the pouring. Traditionally it is eaten with a pickle and a sip of coffee to cut the sweetness.

Planning for 600? Count on roughly 60 ml of syrup per person, or about 36 litres in total.
